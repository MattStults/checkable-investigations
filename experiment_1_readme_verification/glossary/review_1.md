## Verdict

The glossary is a serious, largely accurate artifact — its git, ledger, redaction and prereg-integrity conventions reproduce exactly against the repo (656 commits, 620 trailers with the right split, one deleting hunk at +89/−121, the sidecar hash matching pre-`6d01415b` and not after), and its habit of listing conflicts rather than resolving them is the right instinct. But it is **not fit to freeze after should-fix items alone**, because four findings are blocking and two of them are the failure the experiment is designed to exclude: two phrases in the glossary — "the loop iterated against" and "misses printed by name" — occur nowhere in the 656-commit record except in the README under test, so translators would be carrying the README's own gloss of Q01 and its own answer to Q06 into the record they are supposed to read blind; and the `Q04`/`Q32` operationalisations are lifted from the frozen inventory, telling the translator which question is being asked. The other two blockers are structural rather than contaminating: the `raw` tier merges two incompatible record senses inside a closed equality-matching enum, and there is no disposition rule that would make Q01's arc-2 handoff figures come out as anything but `tier=stated`, so the inventory's most load-bearing claim would silently fail to match. Fix those four, add the separability/null and engine-failure entries (without which Q19/Q20/Q22/Q28/Q39 and H08/V02 have no vocabulary at all), correct the four miscitations and the "28 terms"/"All citations resolve" overclaims, and the artifact is freezable; as it stands a freeze would bake a README-derived phrase and a pre-resolved tier into the blind arm.
